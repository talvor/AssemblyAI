Roles and workflow homes

Coordination owns the user conversation, intake routing, and end-to-end delivery progress. It presents questions and updates through Lavish and tracks work across roles.
• Planning owns requirements, decision maps, and specifications. Its workflow homes include wayfinder, grilling, domain-modeling, to-spec, and to-tickets. It prepares and interprets planning questions; Coordination carries the live exchange with the user.
• Research owns evidence gathering and research workflows. Findings inform decisions; they do not substitute for human answers.
• Engineering owns implementation, technical diagnosis, and execution of technical prototypes. Planning coordinates UI prototype exploration, with Engineering workers producing the artifact for human review. Operational work initially sits with Engineering rather than a sixth Operations role.
• Quality owns independent review and validation, including code-review workflows. Its assessment remains distinct from Engineering's implementation responsibility.

These are responsibility boundaries, not five mandatory sequential stages for every job. Skills remain reusable methods, not exclusive permissions. Exact default skill bundles and adoption/custody of no-mistakes remain separate decisions.

Leaders across concurrent jobs

Each role has exactly one leader across the factory, serving all jobs and repositories with separate context for each job. Concurrent jobs do not create another set of leaders. Leaders delegate concurrent execution to workers. Scheduling, capacity, process persistence, and failover mechanisms remain later decisions.

Coordination is the single user-facing counterpart. The relevant leader owns the substance of its questions and decisions; Coordination faithfully carries the exchange and returns the user's answers. Agents cannot supply the human side of grilling.

Worker ownership and lifetime

Each worker has one owning leader and one bounded assignment at a time. Other leaders can provide information, but scope, priority, and cancellation changes go through the owning leader. Cross-role work is requested leader-to-leader; the receiving leader chooses its workers.

Workers are assignment-scoped. The owning leader accepts or rejects the result against the assignment's criteria. Rejected work may return to the same worker for correction. Acceptance ends the assignment; later work requires an explicit new assignment. Runtime-session reuse is optional and must preserve job isolation and the new ownership boundary. This does not select process shutdown or recovery behavior.

Handoff and completion contract

A handoff identifies the job, requested outcome, relevant decisions and evidence, constraints and already-granted permissions, acceptance criteria, and accountable receiving leader. The receiver explicitly accepts, requests clarification, or declines with a reason.

Results link to artifacts and evidence and disclose unresolved gaps. The receiving leader checks the result; a message saying "done" alone is insufficient. Coordination tracks end-to-end progress. Storage schemas and message protocols remain with the coordination-state decision.

Disagreements and changes

Leaders first reconcile disagreements against agreed requirements and evidence within their domains. Coordination owns unresolved cross-role trade-offs and routes decisions requiring human judgment back to the user. It cannot mark failed validation as passed.

Scope changes return to Planning and affected owning leaders. Workers receive assignment changes through their owners. Exact delegated authority and permission rules remain with the authority-and-escalation decision.

Leader execution boundary

Leaders can inspect context, reason, converse, accept results, and maintain coordination records themselves. Substantive research, implementation, prototype creation, and independent validation run as bounded worker assignments.
