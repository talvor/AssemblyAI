Confirmed adapter contract

  1. Keep the native interactive CLI. A terminal relay owns the PTY; provider-specific passive hooks report lifecycle observations. The user reviews hook trust and answers native permission prompts.
  2. One input owner and one current assignment per worker session. Human takeover fences automated input. Pause or interrupt at a known boundary, reconcile any draft or in-flight turn, record the intervention, then explicitly release control. Detach alone does not resume automation.
  3. Separate process, turn, decision and assignment state. A Stop hook can carry a result, a blocked report or a human-decision request. Require an explicit structured envelope with assignment/decision identity and independent owner acceptance. Silence, exit or a success-looking screen never establishes completion.
  4. Pause when evidence is ambiguous. Missing or conflicting signals mean unknown/needs reconciliation. Never infer idle from a timeout, retry side effects blindly, or redispatch an unaccepted assignment merely because a terminal stopped.
  5. Retain the selected access and fallback rules. Check native subscription readiness without copying credentials; readiness is not a quota guarantee. After dispatch, pause access failures and ask before changing provider. No API-billing fallback. Before-dispatch selection still requires qualified compatible providers.
  6. Qualify each version/configuration deliberately. These exact local probes establish feasibility only. Reject or pause unsupported combinations rather than claiming compatibility from a version string.

What remains unproven

Remote hosts; provider outages; expired, revoked or exhausted subscriptions; actual fallback; lost/duplicate/reordered hooks; crash recovery; durable result correlation; and safe takeover during active generation or a half-written prompt. Claude’s structured interruption acknowledgment needs qualification beyond the observed transcript error. A late Codex rate-limit dialog intercepted automated input, demonstrating that PTY write success is not dispatch acceptance. Native modal handling and positive submission correlation are also launch qualification requirements. No exactly-once side-effect guarantee is claimed.

These are material limits. The current relay is throwaway code, not a production supervisor. A production adapter must keep affected work paused whenever it cannot establish a trustworthy state.

Where the remaining decisions live

Define v1 acceptance scenarios and evidence must require the untested failure and remote scenarios before launch certification.

This decision resolves local feasibility and unblocks hosting. It does not certify launch readiness or settle downstream designs.
