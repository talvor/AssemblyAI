Is this the resolution to record?

This is everything settled in this ticket across three rounds, including both of your notes. On confirmation I post it as the resolution of Define operating limits and visibility, close the ticket, add a line to the map's Decisions so far, update the map's Not yet specified and Out of scope sections as described below, and keep the board, your answers and the glossary change for archiving. This is a planning decision and authorizes no implementation.

  1. Budgets: the spending budget is zero by default, so any action that would spend money needs your grant naming the amount and scope. Allowance, the usage your subscriptions permit in rolling windows, is not budgeted per job: worker caps pace it, and the rules below apply when a provider reports its limit. AsmAI records token counts per dispatch and shows each provider's allowance used and reset times in status. There is no reserve: the factory uses allowance until the provider stops it.
  2. Concurrency: each provider has a cap on workers whose dispatch is in progress, 3 for Claude and 3 for Codex by default. Workers waiting on their leader, on you or on a hold, and a paused job's workers, do not count, and their sessions stay alive. Leaders do not count. There is no cap on jobs or per repository. An assignment that finds its provider's cap full is queued, first come first served across jobs, shown as queued to its leader and in the status line. It waits for its configured provider and never overflows to the other one.
  3. Leaders on demand: Coordination always runs while the factory runs. The daemon starts any other role's leader when a message for that role arrives and no leader is running, so Coordination ensures a leader by handing work to its role and never manages processes. The daemon stops a leader at a boundary, after a 10-minute idle grace, once its role has no open work in any unpaused job: no open handoffs to it, no assignments it owns, no decision it requested still pending, and nothing in its inbox. The daemon knows all of this from its store. A role with no running leader and no work is normal, not a fault. At most one leader per role still holds, and a crashed leader with open work still restarts, up to 3 times within 15 minutes, before its role holds.
  4. A leader's provider: a leader that starts while its role has no open work in any job, paused or not, counts as before first dispatch. It may start on the other configured, compatible provider automatically, recorded and shown in status, and returns to its configured provider at a later start once access is back. While its role has open work, changing its provider needs your decision.
  5. Allowance used up: agents on that provider stop at their boundary and hold. At the reported reset time AsmAI resumes each on the same provider with a new dispatch and tells its owning leader. Queued assignments not yet dispatched may start on the other configured, compatible provider; otherwise they wait for the reset. A leader with open work on that provider leaves its role waiting until the reset, and Coordination, or the status line if Coordination itself is held, offers you the provider change.
  6. Provider failures: when a provider positively reports that a turn ended on an error (Claude Code's StopFailure hook; Codex's signal is still to be qualified), the daemon resumes the same live session with a new dispatch, up to 5 times, waiting 30 seconds and doubling to at most 8 minutes. An unexplained stop is still unknown. Past that bound, or while the provider stays down, the provider is marked unavailable: its agents hold, queued work may use the other provider before first dispatch, and the daemon rechecks every 5 minutes and resumes held agents when it answers. A lost or expired sign-in holds the same way; the status line, catch-up and status show the native sign-in command, and work resumes when a recheck sees sign-in restored. AsmAI never starts a login. A pinned CLI that fails to start follows the leader crash rule for a leader, and a worker's assignment needs reconciliation.
  7. Work that keeps failing: an assignment that reaches 5 dispatches without acceptance holds, and its owning leader escalates to you with what was tried, while the rest of the job and other jobs continue. Only dispatches that redo or correct the work count: a rejection, a re-dispatch chosen in reconciliation, and a new dispatch after you typed during an intervention. Resumptions after an allowance reset, a reported provider error, a pause or asmai stop do not count. A side-effect-free assignment is retried automatically at most twice.
  8. Pause: asmai job pause dispatches nothing new for the job. Running turns finish to their boundary and stop, and the job's workers keep their sessions without counting against the cap. Queued assignments stay queued. Pending decisions stay answerable, and answers apply on resume. Leaders do not act on the job until it resumes, and its work does not keep a leader running.
  9. Resume: each stopped assignment continues with a new dispatch after its owning leader reloads the job's brief. Resume also lifts a hold on the job's work once the hold's cause has cleared.
  10. Cancel: asmai job cancel asks you to confirm, then stops all of the job's work at a boundary (--now interrupts instead), withdraws its pending decisions, ends its assignments as cancelled, and reports effects, pushed branches and any open PR. The PR is left open for you to close, and the job ends.
  11. Who pauses and cancels: you, with asmai job pause|resume|cancel, or by telling Coordination, which acts citing your witnessed message. No other agent pauses or cancels a whole job. There is no factory-wide pause beyond asmai stop.
  12. Timeouts: a working dispatch with no correlated observation for 30 minutes becomes unknown. asmai stop drains for up to 10 minutes, then interrupts the rest.
  13. While you are away: v1 has no notifications and no browser dashboard. Holds and other events appear in the status line, the catch-up when you return, and asmai status. Both features are recorded as v2 candidates in the map's Out of scope section. The notification design from round 1 is kept here for v2: a notify command you configure, which the daemon runs on the host when something needs you or a job ends, sent only while no terminal is attached, batched, carrying the event, job number and title and a decision's title, never code or diffs, with any credentials in your command rather than in AsmAI.
  14. Health: asmai status shows the daemon and its version, platform certification, each leader (running, stopped with no work, restarting, held), workers in progress against each provider's cap, queued assignments, each provider's state (ready with allowance used and reset times, held until a reset, unavailable, signed out), the Lavish server, paused jobs and holds with their causes, and disk use. asmai status --check exits non-zero when anything needs you, so you can wire it into your own monitoring, for example over SSH. The daemon checks periodically that the Lavish server is alive (restarting it), provider sign-in, free disk and leader liveness, and reports problems through the status line, the catch-up and status.
  15. Disk and host: there are no per-job quotas. Status reports disk use by clones, workspaces, kept workspaces, recordings and the store. Below 5 GB free on the state directory's volume, the daemon creates no new workspaces (their assignments hold) and continues when space returns. You remove kept workspaces with asmai job clean <n>, which is refused while the job is open. There are no CPU or memory limits; the worker caps are the control.
  16. Logs: the daemon's operational log rotates through 5 files of 20 MB and is read with asmai log [--follow]. Every agent terminal is recorded per dispatch, compressed, replayed with asmai replay <agent> [--dispatch <id>], and deleted 14 days after its job ends. Provider transcripts stay where the provider writes them, and the journal records their location per dispatch. Nothing records environment variables. The journal stays append-only and is kept in full.
  17. Settings: every number above is a factory-wide setting in the configuration file, shown by asmai config show. The field names stay in the configuration-schema fog.
  18. Changes to earlier decisions: Choose factory hosting and lifecycle assumed every role's leader runs all the time; now only Coordination does, and other leaders run on demand as above. The factory's own stops that earlier tickets called pauses, such as a role after repeated leader crashes or an agent at unknown input, are holds.
  19. Records: the glossary gains Allowance and Hold. No ADR is proposed, because none of these choices is hard to reverse.
  20. Deferred to Define v1 acceptance scenarios and evidence: qualifying, per provider version, the allowance figures (Claude Code's five-hour and seven-day status-line figures, Codex's session-log rate limits), the reported-error signals (Claude Code's StopFailure and Codex's equivalent), resuming after a reported error, holding and resuming at a reset, before-first-dispatch fallback on a limited provider including a leader's on-demand start, leaders starting when a message arrives and stopping after the idle grace, the free-space floor, and terminal recording and replay.

The browser-dashboard fog item is removed, and the Out of scope section gains one line: notifications while you are away and a browser dashboard are v2 candidates. The configuration-schema fog item notes that the operating-limit settings and their defaults are settled here.
